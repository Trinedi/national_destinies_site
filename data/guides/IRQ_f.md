## Concept

Iraq restores the Sawad, the irrigated land between the Tigris and the
Euphrates that once yielded roughly a fifth of the revenue of the entire
Caliphate, as a sovereign Muslim state ruled from Baghdad.

This is not a dead-empire revival. The Jalayirids kept a real court in
Baghdad through the 1330s and well beyond. What died was not the polity
but the water. The Nahrawan canal was breached during a civil war in 937
and never properly repaired, and that collapse, rather than the Mongol
sack of 1258, is why Mesopotamia stopped being the richest tax province
in the Islamic world and became a border march of whoever held Tabriz,
Cairo or Istanbul.

So the whole country runs on water. Not economy in the abstract: specific
canals, specific weirs, and a tax system that makes the treasury care
whether they silt up.

## Forming it

1. **Follow a Muslim religion.**
2. **Be Arab-cultured, or rule from Iraq al-Arabi.** Either qualifies.
   The Jalayirids come in through the second.
3. **Own Baghdad, Basra and Kufa.** The capital, the head of the Gulf,
   and the shrine country. All three are in the south.
4. **Keep your capital inside the required territory.**
5. **Hold 50 percent of Iraq al-Arabi and the Jazira.**
6. **Be at peace with yourself.** A civil war blocks formation.
7. **Form Iraq.**

The fraction is low because Iraq al-Arabi alone is 56 locations.
Consolidating the south clears the bar on its own, which keeps Iraq a
lower-Mesopotamian state. The Jazira counts toward the total without
being required, so the upper country is claimable rather than compulsory.

## Pick your founding doctrine on formation

Al-Mansur's city was two things at once, and they did not fall together.
You are choosing which one you rebuild.

- **Dar al-Khilafa, the House of the Caliphate.** Baghdad as the
  political capital of the Muslim world: prestige, legitimacy, control
  and holy war score. It costs you tolerance of heretics, because a Sunni
  caliphal restoration reads as a threat in Najaf and Hilla, which are
  inside your own borders.
- **Madinat al-Salam, the City of Peace.** Baghdad as the civilizational
  capital: research, literacy and institutions, with better tolerance of
  heathens because the translation movement ran on Christian, Sabian and
  Jewish scholars. It costs manpower. The scholar-city does not fill its
  barracks.

## What happens on formation

- Capital moves to Baghdad.
- Your primary culture becomes Iraqi. No country on the 1337 map starts
  with it, so this is an outcome of forming rather than a requirement.
- Rank rises to kingdom if it was lower.
- **Your pops are left alone.** Forming Iraq converts nobody.
  Mesopotamia stayed plural throughout this period, with Kurdish,
  Turkoman, Syriac and Mandean communities alongside the Arab majority.

## After forming

The heritage tree runs on the canals. Age 1 opens the Nahrawan
head-works. Age 3 replaces fixed-area assessment with muqasama
crop-share, taxing land by how the water is lifted: half rate for
flood-fed ground, a third under a wheel, a quarter under an animal. Your
treasury then loses money when a canal silts, which is the only
arrangement that ever made a state dredge. Age 5 brings the Hindiyya
channel and a zero-sum dispute: let the new Najaf channel run and the
Hilla branch dies, or build the regulator at the fork and pay in coin.

Destiny follows your formation choice:

- **Dar al-Khilafa.** The caliphal road, through the Zagros gates at
  Kermanshah and Hamadan, then Aleppo and Damascus, then Cairo and
  Jerusalem to seat a shadow caliph. Capstone is the **Dar al-Barid**,
  the caliphal post relay, with a resident intelligencer reporting on the
  governor next door.
- **Madinat al-Salam.** Water as machinery: Maragheh's observatory
  instruments, Shushtar's Sasanian weirs, then the corrected tables at
  Samarkand. Capstones are the **Dulab Works**, a yard building norias
  and al-Jazari pumps, and the **Marsad** observatory.

## Notes

- **Bureaucracies.** Four, of which you run two. The **mirab and the
  nawba** administer the timed water turn and call the yearly canal
  clearance. The **soyurghal** is the Mongol grant in perpetuity inherited
  from the Jalayirids, which hardened into hereditary tax-immune holdings.
  Those two pull against each other: the mirab raises your rural control
  ceiling and a neglected soyurghal collapses it. The others are the
  **jahbadhiyya**, the Jewish court bankers, and the **naqib al-ashraf**,
  the register of the Prophet's descendants.
- **Iraq leads on into the Khilafah**, continuing the Dar al-Khilafa road.
  A Shia Iraq cannot go that way.
- The Bayt al-Hikma, the Hajj and the caliphate belong to the Khilafah, so
  Iraq reaches for the **Mustansiriya** instead, a real building still
  standing, and buildable in Baghdad and nowhere else.
