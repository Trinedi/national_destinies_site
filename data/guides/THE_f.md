## Concept

**Thessalonica already exists in vanilla EU5 and has never had any content
until now.** It was added in a later patch and this mod never covered it.

It is the smallest formable in the mod, level 1, holding one great city
and the Macedonian and Thessalian hinterland. Do not expect an empire.

Its identity is a single fact: **Thessalonica had three masters, and none
of them was the crown.** The Lombard baronage that Boniface of Montferrat's
knights founded in 1204. The guilds and dockhands of the harbour, who ran
the city as an independent commune for eight years. And the Orthodox
church, with the Holy Mountain behind it. Every choice in this tag comes
back to that triangle.

## Forming it

**Requirements.** You must be a Frankokratia state, which means a French,
Iberian or Italian culture group **and** Catholic (or a non-Orthodox
Christian from age 4) **and** having held one of the fifteen Latin tags of
Greece. You must own **Thessaloniki**, and you must be in **age 1 or age
2**. Achaea and the Latin Empire are explicitly blocked. Territory is the
six provinces of Macedonia and Thessaly at 80 percent.

**The age cap is the real constraint.** This formable closes at age 3. If
you want it, you are building for it from the opening.

Note that Albania is the one Frankokratia tag with a formable definition
of its own (level 2), so the level 1 gate excludes it. Every other
candidate carries no tier and qualifies.

## Why the timing works out the way it does

Age 2 begins in **1342**, which is the exact year the Zealots seized
Thessalonica and began eight years of communal rule.

So the formation window splits cleanly in two. Age 1, from 1337 to 1341,
is the Latin inheritance: Boniface's kingdom, the baronage, the claim.
Age 2 is the crisis decade. The content is built on that split rather
than around it.

## Pick your founding doctrine on formation

You are choosing which of the three masters to rule through, and every
option surrenders something real. Forming is not pure upside.

- **Rule through the barons.** Crown estate power, garrisons, noble levies
  and demesne produce. The burghers resent it. Pushes toward aristocracy.
- **Rule through the commune.** Burgher satisfaction, urban growth, sea
  trade and sailors. You surrender your grip on your own capital and cut
  the barons out entirely. Pushes toward plutocracy.
- **Rule through the church.** Toleration of the Greek rite, devotion,
  willing Greek levies and control, at the cost of your own religion's
  tolerance, because the realm's own Latins take it as betrayal.

## What happens on formation

Rank rises to kingdom if below, preserving vanilla's own effect.

## After forming

**Six vanilla advances already exist** for this tag in the Frankokratia
file (the Fair of Saint Demetrios, the Lombard Guard, the Macedonian fur
trade, the Thessalonican crown, the University of Thessalonica and the Via
Egnatia restoration), so the mod's tree deliberately builds around them
rather than over them.

The centrepiece is **the Zealots hold the gates**, and it offers all three
historically attested exits: charter the commune and lose crown
authority; storm the lower town and hang the council, which is what
Kantakouzenos actually did in 1350, earning permanent burgher hatred; or
wait, because the city turned on the Zealots itself in 1349, at the cost
of eight years of looking helpless.

The other events run through Athos appealing to Rome under Innocent III's
1214 bull, **Gregory Palamas barred from his own see in 1347** by the
Zealots (with the option a Byzantine emperor never had: declare the see
Latin), the Venetian handover of 1423, the Sephardic arrival of 1492, the
Union of Florence proclaimed from the ambo of Saint Demetrios or quietly
shelved, the Gedaliah press of 1512, and the twelve mining villages asking
for their charter.

Buildings are bound to what is physically in those six provinces: the
Eptapyrgion citadel whose guns face down into its own city, the
Metochion, the Sidirokausia silver and lead mines of the Chalkidiki, the
Hall of the Myron (Thessaloniki's map good is literally medicaments), the
Broadcloth Hall, the Argyrokopeion mint, and the Krokos House (Servia's
map good is saffron).

Destiny takes the two ends of the same argument:

- **The barons' road.** The two Slav rocks that strangled the historical
  kingdom, Prosek and Melnik, then the reckoning with the Epirote
  despotate that destroyed it in 1224, then the Parliament of Ravennika
  where Attica owed it homage in 1209. Capstone is the **Castellany of
  Platamon**, the Frankish tower at the mouth of the Vale of Tempe.
- **The commune's road.** Three island ports rather than a land campaign:
  the alum of Lemnos, the fondaco at Negroponte, and the Maona of Chios.
  Capstone is the **House of the Kommerkion**, the customs house on the
  mole.

Neither path reaches past Greece and the Aegean, which is deliberate for a
level 1 country.

The church option has no destiny branch.

## Notes

**Bureaucracies.** Four, of which you run two, and there is **no crown
estate anywhere in the file**. All alignments are "my body likes it, the
other two resent it", because there was no crown party in this city. The
**Curia of the Lombards**, the court of fief holders who shut the gates on
Henry of Flanders in 1207 and offered the crown to William VI of
Montferrat. The **Archon of the Demos**, the elected magistracy of the
waterfront. The **Synaxis of Karyes**, the assembly of Athonite abbots
fixed by Tzimiskes' typikon of 972. And the **Sekreton of Thessalonike**,
the crown's own bench, instituted by Empress Yolanda Irene of Montferrat
after she took up residence in the city in 1303.

That last one is worth a note. Taking it means seating only one master
instead of two, which sharpens the whole premise. And its decay mechanic
was written by history: **in 1337, the game's start year, three of the
four judges of that tribunal were convicted of taking bribes and exiled.**

**A Montferrat connection runs through this tag and two others in the
batch.** Boniface of Montferrat founded this kingdom in 1204 and his son
Demetrius held it to 1224, and the Palaiologos marquises of Montferrat
later endowed its royal court. If you are playing Montferrat itself, note
that you cannot form Thessalonica: Montferrat is not one of the fifteen
Frankokratia tags. See the Sardinia-Piedmont guide for what Montferrat
can do instead.
