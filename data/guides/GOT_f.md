## Concept

The Regnum Gothorum restores the Visigothic kingdom of Toledo, and it is
the best-grounded revival in the mod, because neogoticismo was not
nostalgia. It was the load-bearing legitimising ideology of the Asturian
and Castilian monarchy. The claim to be continuing the Gothic kingdom is
precisely what made the Reconquista a *restoration* rather than a
conquest, and it is why the Leonese kings minted themselves *Imperator
totius Hispaniae*.

The institutions this country is built on genuinely survived the
kingdom's fall in 711. The Liber Iudiciorum stayed in force as the Fuero
Juzgo. Toledo kept ecclesiastical primacy over Iberia. The Councils of
Toledo remained the reference for how a crown and a church legislate
together. What did not survive was the elective kingship, and that
absence is the thing the whole tree is organised around.

## Forming it

**Requirements.** Castilian or Leonese primary culture, and a Christian
religion (the group rather than Catholic specifically, so a reformed
crown can still argue the succession through the councils and the code).
You must own **Toledo, Granada and Narbonne**, not be in a civil war, and
have no active disaster. Territory is the whole Iberian region plus
Septimania proper (the Narbonnais, Nimois and Razes provinces) at 75
percent.

**Narbonne is the only genuinely binding requirement.** By the time you
have 75 percent of Iberia you will hold Toledo and Granada anyway. Narbonne
forces a trans-Pyrenean war that no ordinary Iberian unification ever
needs, and that is the point: the pre-Vouille borders are what make this
shape something other than Spain with different words.

The no-disaster clause protects vanilla's Castilian civil war, whose
`can_start` is scoped to Castile and would be stranded by a mid-arc tag
switch. It also reads in fiction: the Visigothic elective succession was
murderous enough that Frankish writers called regicide the *morbus
Gothorum*, so the restored kingdom is not proclaimed over a contested
crown.

## The level, which matters more here than usual

**Regnum Gothorum is level 3, exactly the same as Spain, and that is
deliberate.** The engine only lets a country form a formable of strictly
higher level, so Spain can never form the Regnum Gothorum and the Regnum
Gothorum can never form Spain.

They are mutually exclusive destinies for Castile. This is not a ladder.
Choosing the Gothic crown means giving up the Reconquista-and-colonies
Spain permanently, and vice versa. Decide before you form.

## Pick your founding doctrine on formation

The two options are the two available answers to what the restored crown
actually *is*, and the requester's original suggestion of an alternate
name turns out to be exactly the right axis.

- **Regnum Gothorum.** The restored state as a juridical and
  ecclesiastical person: the councils, the code, the primacy of Toledo.
  Legislative efficiency, religious influence, integration speed,
  literacy and heretic tolerance. It pays with reduced crown estate
  power, because a crown that rules through the councils does not rule
  alone.
- **Reiki Gutane.** A people rather than an office, a king held by the
  oath of his sworn men. Manpower, land morale, legitimacy, cultural
  tradition and noble satisfaction. It pays in diplomatic reputation,
  because Latin Christendom hears the Gothic name as an archaism.

The reconstruction is sound rather than invented: *reiki* is Wulfila's
Gothic word for rule, and *gutane* is the genitive plural attested on the
Pietroassa ring.

## What happens on formation

Your rank rises to empire, which is not inflation for its own sake. The
Leonese imperial title was the institutional form of the Gothic
succession claim: Sancho the Great minted himself Emperor of all Spain,
and Alfonso VII was crowned to it at Leon in 1135 on the explicit
doctrine that whoever held Leon was the direct heir of the Gothic kings.

Your capital moves to Toledo.

## After forming

The heritage tree spends four institutional hooks. The **Fuero Juzgo**,
where the interesting fact is not that the code survived but that it was
*territorial*, applying one law to Goths and Romans alike rather than
personal law by ethnicity. The **Councils of Toledo** as a joint
church-and-crown assembly. **Toledo's primacy** over all Iberia. And the
**Guarrazar goldsmith tradition**, from the votive crowns found near
Toledo.

The tree has **no naval content at all**, which is a stated design
choice: three Iberian formables already own galleys, and a kingdom that
has to fight for a mountain corridor to exist is a land power.

The destiny tree resolves the question the heritage tree deliberately
refuses to answer. Canon 75 of Toledo IV in 633 made the kingship
elective and anathematised regicide, and the practice produced the
*morbus Gothorum*. Each path closes it in a different direction:

- **The crown as an elective office.** Canon 75 taken literally, with the
  assembly of magnates and bishops as a real electorate (which is why the
  Septimanian sees have to come in, since the canon says "the bishops of
  the whole people"). Then the Eighth Council's 653 ruling separating the
  **Thesaurus Publicus**, the treasury of the kingdom, from the king's
  own chamber. Its capstone has **no conquest condition at all**: it is
  bought with the enlightenment, gold, development and prestige.
- **The crown as blood and oath.** Resolved by the one mechanism that
  ever beat a Gothic election, association of the heir in his father's
  lifetime, as Chindasuinth did for Recceswinth in 649. Its objectives
  follow the *gens* rather than the office: the strait Julian of Ceuta
  opened in 711, Theodoric's Italy, and finally the Crimean Goths at
  Theodoro, where the game itself assigns Gothic culture and where
  Busbecq wrote down 86 words of living Gothic in 1562. Capstone is the
  **Thiudangardi**, Wulfila's word for the king's enclosure, a household
  rather than an office, and gated to anywhere except the capital.

## Notes

**Bureaucracies.** Four, of which you run two. The **numerarii** of the
fisc, appointed from court for one-year terms and answerable to an annual
provincial synod. The **episcopal schools**, from canon 1 of Toledo II in
527, the earliest cathedral school on record in the Latin West. The
**general oath of fidelity**, whose neutral effect permanently suppresses
noble power because Recceswinth's code voids any oath sworn to a lord
other than the king. And the **conventus vicinorum**, the public assembly
of the neighbours, the only jurisdiction the Liber places below the
count's court.

Two of them are deliberately coupled on one lever: neglecting the
numerarii drives free cultivators to put themselves under protectors,
and funding the assembly pulls the same key back. Running both means the
village shields the peasantry from the collectors, which is what the
councils spent two centuries legislating about.

**The anti-feudal oath is the file's clearest find-and-replace failure.**
A Frankish or Castilian realm was built out of exactly the private oaths
that Visigothic law annulled, so that modifier set would be wrong on any
other country in the game.

**Occitania overlaps on the Septimanian coastal corridor.** Nothing in
Occitania requires Narbonne, so the two can coexist as claims, but the
Regnum Gothorum hard-requires it and will have to take it.
