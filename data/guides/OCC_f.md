## Concept

**Occitania already exists in vanilla EU5 and has never had any content
until now.** It was added in a later patch and this mod simply never
covered it. It is also the single most requested item on the Workshop,
asked for in its own discussion thread and separately by three more
commenters.

Occitania is a country of **written instruments**. Charters, fors,
costumas, notarial acts, poetic laws, consular oaths and provincial
estates. It is the one polity in this period that deliberately
standardised its own vernacular as both a literary and a legal language.
Its wealth is essentially one dyestuff. Its politics is a permanent
negotiation between two crowns. And its religion is a suppression it
never forgot, which is exactly why the next dissent found a home there.

## Forming it

**Requirements.** Occitan or Gascon dialect. That is the whole potential
gate, and the allow block is empty. Territory is Provence and Languedoc
plus fifteen provinces spanning the Gascon south, the Auvergne and the
Limousin, at 75 percent.

There is no city requirement, which makes this an unusually clean
formable to plan around: it is purely a question of holding
three quarters of the Occitan-speaking world.

## Catharism, handled honestly

Three of the four requesters asked for Cathar content, and it is the
obvious hook. Here is why the content treats it the way it does.

**There is no living Cathar church in 1337.** The Albigensian Crusade
ended in 1229, Montsegur fell in 1244, and the Inquisition finished the
job: Guillaume Belibaste, the last known Cathar perfectus in the
Languedoc, was burned in 1321, sixteen years before the game starts.

So Catharism appears as **memory, grievance and confiscated property**,
never as a faith you can restore. The age 1 advance grants heretic
tolerance with a devotion penalty, and its event is about the crusade
confiscation rolls: reopen them, and the crown gains while the planted
northern lineages lose, or seal them and buy the church's peace. The
habit of sheltering the hunted then carries forward mechanically into the
Edict of Castres and the Camisards, which is the real historical
continuity rather than an invented revival.

## What is actually alive at the 1337 start

Far better material, and all of it in period:

- **The Consistori del Gay Saber**, founded at Toulouse in 1323, fourteen
  years before the start date. A living literary academy running the Jocs
  Florals and awarding the violeta d'aur.
- **The Leys d'Amors**, Guilhem Molinier's codification of Occitan grammar
  and poetics, completed in 1341, squarely inside the game window.
- **The capitouls of Toulouse** and the wider consulate tradition, genuine
  municipal self-government that survived to 1789.
- **Pays de droit ecrit**: the Midi kept Roman written law against the
  customary law of the north.
- **Montpellier's medical faculty** (papal charter 1220) and the
  **University of Toulouse** (1229), the latter founded as a condition of
  the Treaty of Paris explicitly to combat heresy, and then becoming the
  Midi's own law school.
- **Gaston III Febus** of Foix-Bearn, who played England and France
  against each other to keep his lands effectively independent.

## Pick your founding doctrine on formation

Not war against trade. The question is **where sovereignty comes from**,
which is the Midi's actual constitutional argument.

- **Lo Pacte Consular**, Toulouse's answer. Burgher satisfaction,
  legislative efficiency, control, trade and building efficiency, bought
  with a heavy cut to crown estate power and noble satisfaction.
- **Tenc de Degun**, Febus at Orthez in 1347: "held of God and of no man
  in the world". Legitimacy, peace negotiation, garrisons, cheaper wars
  and prestige, bought with diplomatic reach and burgher satisfaction.

Both pay for themselves out of an estate, which is the point.

## What happens on formation

Rank rises to kingdom, not empire. State markers set, mild stability.

## After forming

The heritage tree is the largest in this batch at 41 advances, running
from the Consistori and the Leys d'Amors through bastide charters and
pareage, transhumance and the dralhas, Sainte-Cecile of Albi, the
Parlement of Toulouse of 1443, the pastel boom and Jean de Bernuy standing
surety for Francois I, alum of Millau, the 1598 indigo prohibition,
article 111 of Villers-Cotterets, the Chambre de l'Edit at Castres,
Cevennes silk, Armagnac, the echelles du Levant, Riquet's canal,
Montpellier verdigris, the Camisards, the cadets de Gascogne and finally
the Felibrige, whose orthographic split closes the arc the Leys d'Amors
opened.

There is a real multi-stage economy behind it: woad to coques to agranat
using Millau alum, then Languedoc cloth, then londrins seconds for the
Aleppo trade, plus verdigris, Armagnac brandy and Rouergue steel. The
chain is internally consistent good for good, so each stage genuinely
consumes what the last one made.

Destiny continues each formation answer outward:

- **The consular road.** Credit first, through the Cahorsin lenders, then
  the Atlantic outlet via the Bordeaux wine privilege, then one sworn
  instrument covering every consulate and valley including the Limousin
  and Auvergne. Capstone is the **Fiera Franca**, a chartered free fair
  with its own peace and law-merchant court.
- **The Orthez road.** Hold the western doors at Bayonne and
  Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port, inherit Navarre at Pamplona and Tudela, and
  finally settle **the debt of Muret** by holding the Aragonese cradle at
  Jaca and Huesca. Capstone is the **Castel Sobeiran**, a prince's brick
  keep in the Montaner manner, gated to hills or mountains away from the
  capital.

Muret is worth knowing: Peter II of Aragon died there in 1213 fighting
for the Occitan lords against Simon de Montfort. Aragon is the natural
partner or rival for this country, not a stranger.

## Notes

**Bureaucracies.** Four, of which you run three, which is unusually
generous and fits a country defined by its assemblies. The **Estates of
Languedoc**, which voted their own taxation as a lump sum and apportioned
it through the assiettes of the civil dioceses, and crucially borrowed in
the province's own name. The **Cour des Aides** of Montpellier, from 1437,
the one bench that could void a claimed exemption. **Lo Compes**, the
community land register from 1320 that made the taille fall on land
rather than on persons. And the **Cour Majour of Bearn**, twelve barons as
jurats, whose consent was needed for any war outside Bearn and before
whom the prince swore to the Fors before Bearn swore to him.

The Estates deliberately avoid the shape every peer uses. Burgundy-Provence,
Greater Burgundy and the Aragonese Empire all build their representative
body out of parliament mechanics and legislative efficiency. Languedoc's
estates use none of that, because their distinguishing feature is fiscal:
a province that collected its own tax and borrowed on its own credit.

**Nothing in Occitania depends on holding Narbonne.** The Regnum Gothorum,
built in the same batch, hard-requires it, and the two share only the
Septimanian coastal corridor. They are both level 3, so neither can form
the other.
