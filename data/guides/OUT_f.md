## Concept

Outremer is the crusader capstone: the tier that the Kingdom of
Jerusalem, the Latin Empire, Crusader Africa, the Knights of the Temple
and the Sovereign Order of Saint John all feed into.

It is not about conquest. The defining fact of the crusader states was
that a tiny Frankish ruling caste governed an overwhelmingly non-Latin
population: Muslim, Eastern Christian, Jewish and Samaritan. This is a
country about governing a majority you cannot convert and must not try to.

## Forming it

1. **Be Catholic.**
2. **Be a kingdom or an empire**, or have held Jerusalem, the Latin
   Empire, Crusader Africa, the Knights of the Temple or the Sovereign
   Order of Saint John.
3. **Keep Catholics at 50 percent or less of your own population.** You
   may only proclaim Outremer while your own church is a minority among
   your own subjects.
4. **Own Jerusalem, Acre, Cairo, Baghdad, Mosul, Mecca, Medina and Adan.**
   Acre is not decoration: it was the kingdom's capital from 1191 and its
   last mainland holding in 1291.
5. **Hold 60 percent of the Crescent and Egypt, plus the Hijaz, South
   Yemen and Cyprus.** Roughly 278 of 462 locations.
6. **Be at peace with yourself.** A civil war blocks formation.
7. **Form Outremer.**

**The minority requirement is the whole country.** It shuts off the
convert-or-depopulate route to a clean crusader empire, it counts Eastern
Christians, Jews and Samaritans on the governed side rather than only
Muslims, and it excludes a Catholic-majority world empire. A France or
Castile that keeps its metropole and also takes the Middle East cannot
form this. France with a Levantine province is France.

In practice it only bites a player dragging a large Catholic homeland
behind them.

The consequence for play: this is the one Catholic country in the game
that cannot use the Catholic religion tree's real content. There is not
one discipline bonus, conversion-speed modifier or holy war score line
anywhere in the tag. Because each estate's satisfaction and power follow
the crown's tolerance of that estate's faith, tolerance of heathens and
heretics is simply your stability here.

## Pick your founding doctrine on formation

The formation event is a coronation that inherits a problem. The herald
reads out the style, then the chamberlain hands over the survey: fewer
than two in ten hear Mass in Latin, forty frontier castles answer to
Rome, the Italian quarters in your own ports keep their own gallows and
pay nothing, and the revenue funding your army is the poll tax on
subjects who are not Christian.

- **The Compact of the Courts.** Tolerance, cultures capacity,
  separatism, control and estate satisfaction, bought with a heavy cut to
  crown estate power, because you have surrendered jurisdiction.
- **Custodian of the Sanctuaries.** Red Sea trade, tax, trade centre
  power, merchant capacity, supply and reputation, bought with your own
  religion's tolerance, because your own clergy call it apostasy.
- **The New Towns.** Construction, building efficiency, population
  growth, development and assimilation, bought with separatism, because
  settlers are planted among people who did not ask for them.

## What happens on formation

- Capital moves to Jerusalem.
- Rank rises to empire.
- Large stability bonus.

## After forming

Heritage is built out of institutions rather than armies: the *ra'is*
village headmen through whom Frankish lords actually administered
non-Latin communities, the capitation inherited from the Fatimid diwan,
the Maronite crossbow muster, and the fact that the crusader states
introduced no serfdom, native peasants owing rent rather than labour.

Three of its thirty advances carry a permanent downside, which is this
tag's tone. The military orders' castellany gives you the best fortified
frontier in the world and costs crown power forever. The Italian communes
give you trade and merchant capacity and cost tax revenue forever.

Destiny splits into the compact and the sea:

- **The Compact of the Courts** continues into development, institutions
  and jurisdiction, ending at a **Chartered Quarter**, the Venetian and
  Genoese instrument issued by charter instead of extracted by a fleet.
  Its first objective is Cilicia, because Smbat Sparapet's Armenian
  translation of 1265 is the only surviving text of the Assises of
  Antioch. Its last is Sultaniyya, whose Latin archbishopric of 1318 held
  a nominal province covering all of Asia.
- **Custodian of the Sanctuaries** runs the sea that the pilgrimage and
  the pepper both cross: Aydhab and Suakin, then Hormuz and Muscat, then
  Calicut and Diu. The capstone **Granary of the Two Cities** is the
  Egyptian grain endowment that fed Mecca and Medina under four dynasties
  and returned nothing to any of them.

## Notes

- **Bureaucracies.** Six, of which you run three, and they are six
  positions in one argument about who owns the ports and the courts: the
  **Court of the Syrians** under the ra'is, the **Court of the Chain** at
  the harbour, the **Charters of the Communes** from the Pactum Warmundi
  of 1123, the **Office of the Turcopolier** commanding native light
  horse, the **New Towns** from Magna Mahumeria, and the **Restor of the
  Host**, the crown's duty to replace horses lost in service.
- The Turcopolier carries the tag's sharpest modifier, levy size from pops
  of non-accepted culture. Outremer's military problem as one number.
- The New Towns bureaucracy is only available if you took the New Towns
  formation option, which has no other follow-through. Take it and you see
  six institutions for three slots. Otherwise five.
