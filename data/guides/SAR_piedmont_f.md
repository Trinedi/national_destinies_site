## Concept

**Sardinia-Piedmont already exists in vanilla EU5 and has never had any
content until now.** It was added in a later patch and this mod never
covered it. It has zero vanilla advances, so the whole tree is new.

The country is a crown assembled by treaty out of an Alpine duchy and a
Mediterranean island, holding the passes into Italy and governing two
populations under two different legal traditions. That is not a metaphor:
the historical Kingdom of Sardinia exists because Victor Amadeus II
swapped Sicily for Sardinia at The Hague in 1720, having been handed
Sicily at Utrecht in 1713.

Its formation event asks what a crown assembled that way actually rests
on. Geography, law, or descent.

## Forming it

**Requirements.** An Italian culture group primary culture. That is the
entire potential gate, and the allow block is empty. Territory is the
Sardinia and Piedmont areas at 85 percent.

It is a level 2 formable, and it chains naturally toward Italy (level 3),
which is historically exact: Sardinia-Piedmont is the state that actually
unified Italy. Nothing in this content assumes that has happened.

## The Montferrat fork, and why it matters

If you are playing Montferrat, vanilla fires a flavour event that offers
you a choice, and it decides your whole campaign.

Montferrat has been ruled since 1306 by Theodore Palaiologos, son of the
Byzantine Emperor Andronikos II and Yolanda of Montferrat, and the
Palaiologos line held the marquisate until 1533. The event asks whether
you keep the Latin, Catholic, Piedmontese identity your ancestor adopted,
or reclaim the Greek one.

- **Stay Piedmontese.** This is the branch that leads here. You keep an
  Italian culture, so you can form Sardinia-Piedmont, and from there
  Italy.
- **Take the Byzantine court in Italy.** Your country becomes Greek and
  Orthodox. That **permanently locks you out of Sardinia-Piedmont**,
  because the formable gates on the Italian culture group. It also locks
  you out of Thessalonica.

The fork is irreversible by construction and nothing in this content
works around it. What the Greek branch leads to instead is genuinely
larger: a Greek Orthodox Palaiologos Montferrat already satisfies
vanilla's gate to form **Byzantium** (Greek culture group, Orthodox, own
Constantinople), and from Byzantium the Roman restoration opens by the
ordinary rule. That is a full campaign in the other direction.

The Sardinia-Piedmont formation event acknowledges all of this: a
Montferrat former gets a second, distinct description naming Theodore,
Andronikos II, the 1306 inheritance and the line's survival to 1533, and
saying plainly that the family could have carried Constantinople to Italy
and did not. The Latin outcome is framed as the road that was taken, not
as a consolation.

## Pick your founding doctrine on formation

- **Hold the doors of Italy.** Geography as the instrument: movement,
  supply depots, foraging, road building and defensiveness, bought with
  diplomatic reputation, because a state that lives off tolls behind a
  wall is not trusted.
- **Two codes, one registry, one crown.** Law as the instrument:
  legislative efficiency, integration, assimilation, cultures capacity and
  heretic tolerance, bought with noble satisfaction, because codification
  takes the barons' courts away.
- **Rule as the purple ruled.** Only available if you were Montferrat.
  Prestige, diplomatic reputation, cultural tradition, relations and
  legitimacy, bought with cabinet efficiency, because a court that runs on
  descent and ceremony keeps fewer clerks.

## What happens on formation

Vanilla's conditional rank bump to kingdom is preserved exactly: it fires
only if you are not a member of the Holy Roman Empire.

## After forming

The heritage tree runs 31 advances across both halves of the realm, and
its best material is the island's. **Eleanor of Arborea's Carta de Logu**,
promulgated in 1392, is 198 articles in Sardinian setting one penalty
regardless of rank, protecting widows' property, and regulating forest and
livestock. It remained Sardinia's law until 1827, and the age 6 advance
closes that bracket with the Codice Feliciano.

Alongside it: the giudicati courts, Brancaleone Doria's 1391 campaign, the
Cagliari saltpans, the alpine tolls of Mont Cenis and the two St Bernards,
Amadeus VIII's Statuta Sabaudiae of 1430, the failed 1487 crusade against
the Val d'Angrogna, the Reale Udienza, the four-language chancery, the
abolition and restoration of 1536 to 1559, Turin becoming the capital in
1563, the Monti Frumentari grain banks, the ghetto of the Alps and the
Peace of Cavour of 1561, the Glorious Return of 1689, the countermine
galleries and Pietro Micca, Utrecht and The Hague, the Regie Costituzioni
of 1723, the Filatoio Rosso at Caraglio and John Lombe's 1716 theft of its
drawings, and Fenestrelle.

**The Waldensians are worth calling out**, because they are the exact
counterpoint to Occitania in the same batch. Occitania has to treat
Catharism as extinct, because it was. The Waldensians of the Piedmontese
valleys genuinely **survived**, through the 1655 Piedmontese Easter and
beyond, and this tag treats them accordingly. Their levy unit raises only
from Vivaro-Alpine speakers, so it is a minority in arms rather than a
national army.

Destiny continues the two non-Montferrat answers:

- **The passes.** Briancon and Martigny, then a quay of your own at Nice
  and Savona, then the whole arc at Geneva and Genoa. None of those is
  Savoyard at game start. Capstone is the **Ospizio del Valico**, the
  pass-summit hospice of the Bernard of Menthon type, gated to mountains
  with a road, because a pass open in February is worth several open in
  July.
- **The registry.** Two of its three stages gate on institutions and
  development rather than armies, and its targets are law schools: Pavia,
  then Bastia and Corte for a third custom, then Bologna and Padua for one
  bench. Capstone is the **Archivio di Corte**, the purpose-built royal
  archive Victor Amadeus II raised at Turin in 1731.

## Notes

**Bureaucracies.** Four, of which you run two, and two of them are
deliberately opposed: the **intendenti**, Victor Amadeus II's provincial
intendants who broke local and feudal jurisdiction, make revoking
privilege cheap; the **Order of Saints Maurice and Lazarus**, from the
bull of 1572, makes granting it cheap. Picking is a political choice
rather than an additive one. The others are the **Regio Economato**, the
crown administration of vacant benefices under the concordat of 1727,
which carries the regio placet (the right to ignore papal bulls), and the
**Magistrato della Riforma**, which licensed the kingdom's schools.

**Deliberately absent.** Nothing here duplicates the separate Sardinia
formable: no Carabinieri, no Bersaglieri, no Risorgimento, no Cavour, no
coral, no Nuraghi, no watchtowers, no Stamenti. And where Italy owns the
urban setificio and Two Sicilies owns San Leucio, this tag takes the rural
water-powered **throwing** mill instead, which is a different industry
making a different product.
